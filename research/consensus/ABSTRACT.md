# Abstract Drafts

## Target Journal

Journal of Informetrics

## Working Title

Consensus as a Boundary Signal for Local Neighborhood Quality in Multi-Layer Scientific Paper Graphs

## Main Abstract

Multi-layer scientific paper graphs are commonly evaluated with global
clustering metrics, but those metrics do not directly tell us whether a target
paper is placed in the right local research neighborhood. We study whether
layer agreement, operationalized as an all-layer consensus combination rule,
acts as a boundary-sensitive signal for local neighborhood quality. We compare
the all-layer consensus run against sum-based baselines under matched rank budgets, build
fixed banks of target papers whose local neighborhoods shift strongly between
methods, and evaluate those cases with an order-balanced dual-pass review
protocol. Across three corrected review slices, the all-layer consensus run wins 83 of 108
non-tie cases (76.9%) and is especially strong when neighborhood ranks
diverge sharply and the two methods share few top neighbors. The dominant
consensus-win mechanisms are removal of broad context noise and preservation of
material-family or method-family coherence, while baseline wins are rarer and
mainly reflect over-regularization or strong single-cue specificity. Low-k
settings remain ambiguity-heavy, but many of those cases collapse into
conservative ties rather than clean baseline wins. We therefore argue that
consensus should be understood not as a universally superior clustering recipe,
but as a boundary-sensitive reranking rule for local scientific neighborhoods.

## Short Abstract

We show that multi-layer consensus functions as a boundary-sensitive local
reranking signal in scientific paper graphs, winning 83/108 corrected
non-tie neighborhood comparisons under an order-balanced review protocol.
